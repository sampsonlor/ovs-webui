# Passive LLDP observation

LLDP observation is disabled by default. To enable it, append `--lldp-observe-interfaces=<Interface management IDs>` to the existing `ovs-mgrd` invocation. Use a comma-separated list of up to sixteen exact Interface management IDs. The capture runs in the manager's existing Linux network namespace and needs `CAP_NET_RAW` and `AF_PACKET`. The packaged unit already bounds capabilities to include `CAP_NET_RAW` and retains its default socket restrictions. For an opt-in systemd deployment, add `AF_PACKET` while retaining the existing allowed families in the service override.

The manager opens one socket per approved live Ethernet Interface. It installs and locks a fixed receive filter before binding to the Interface. The filter admits only untagged LLDP to the nearest-bridge multicast address `01:80:c2:00:00:0e`. The observer disregards outgoing traffic, requests only that multicast membership and closes it with the socket. The packet tap sees ingress before Open vSwitch consumes it. No packet transmit operation, external discovery daemon, or OVS configuration enters this workflow. [Linux receive path](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.8/net/core/dev.c#L5047-L5099)

A bounded read round checks the Linux name, ifindex and link type before and after collection. The manager verifies the associated OVS management identity and resets capture on a rebind or outage. Cap the collection at 128 frames per round and the cache at 256 active advertisements. A kernel drop or consumed budget makes the capture incomplete. A round with packet loss drops previously retained advertisements that may have been withdrawn and keeps its truncation indication until the capture scope is reset. A failed capture or stale OVS association withholds rows. A complete empty view means that no current advertisements have been observed within this specific capture scope.

The receive timestamp comes from the kernel. A repeat advertisement updates `last_seen_at`, a zero TTL withdraws an entry, and each peer expires at its advertised TTL. The page shows the last collection time separately. Chassis ID, Port ID and System Name are peer claims. Identifiers carry their subtype and encoding, and opaque binary identifiers use hex. Tagged advertisements and other LLDP destination scopes are outside this coverage. The before-and-after link observations qualify the local join. They do not prove remote identity, packet forwarding or uninterrupted link ownership.

List and detail require `inventory.read` and `state.read`. Filters cover Interface, Port, Bridge and text. Pages return up to 50 rows or 40 KiB of item JSON. Sealed cursors bind the caller, permission revision, selection and result. The current-page export includes scope, timestamps, coverage and pagination metadata. Standard and Expert share access rules. This establishes the optional LLDP observation in #25 and contributes to #44.
